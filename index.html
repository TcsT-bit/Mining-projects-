Mining SLP Projects – Contractor Code of Practice & Quality Commitment

1. Purpose

This code commits [Your Company Name] to delivering all mining Social Labour Plan and community projects to the required standard, on time, and with full transparency. The goal is to restore public trust in how mining funds are spent.

2. Quality & Technical Standards

We commit to executing all works strictly to the approved project specification, BOQ, and relevant SANS/SANS-based standards.

  • No substitution of materials, equipment, or methods without written approval from the Engineer/Client
  • All material testing, inspections, and certifications will be conducted by SANAS-accredited laboratories
  • Any work that fails testing or inspection will be rectified at our cost and within agreed timelines

3. Transparency, Reporting & Documentation

  • Weekly progress reports, delivery notes, test results, and site diaries submitted to the client
  • Project file maintained and available for client and community oversight on request
  • All payment claims supported by evidence of actual work completed and verified

4. Anti-Corruption, Ethics & Compliance

  • We do not offer, give, or accept bribes, gifts, or inducements to secure contracts
  • We report any attempt at collusion or interference with QA/QC processes immediately
  • Pricing is based on actual cost + fair margin. No underbidding to compromise quality
  • Full compliance with Mine Health and Safety Act, OHSA, and CIDB regulations

5. Project Delivery & Accountability

  • We complete projects as contracted. Cash flow issues notified 14 days in advance
  • We accept standard retention and commit to full defects liability period
  • CIDB grading, tax clearance, COIDA, and references open for verification

6. Local Empowerment & Community Benefit

  • Prioritize local labor, SMMEs, and suppliers per SLP and procurement targets
  • Participate in community engagement sessions during and after handover
  • Uphold health, safety, and environmental standards to protect workers and community
